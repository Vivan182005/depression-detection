# Text, Transcript and LLM-Based Depression Detection on DAIC-WOZ / E-DAIC

Scope: text-only or text-dominant depression detection on DAIC-WOZ (189 sessions; official split 107/35/47) and E-DAIC (275 sessions; official split 163/56/56), 2016-2026, with emphasis on 2023-2026 LLM work. Our project context, for comparison: Whisper-tiny ASR transcripts, emotion-RoBERTa-large mean-pooled embeddings, ridge, text-only AUC ~0.74; fused AUC 0.764 / accuracy 74.3% (majority 68.8%), speaker-disjoint nested CV, N=272.

Method note: full text was read (PDF text extraction or HTML) for Burdisso 2024, Watawana 2026, Ishikawa & Duke 2026, Merzougui 2025, Gomez-Zaragoza 2025, Alhanai 2018, Mandal (QuestMF), PsyGAT 2026, Lee 2026 and Teferra 2025. Rows marked "(via table in X)" are numbers as reported in another paper's comparison table, not checked against the original paper.

## Q1. Best honestly evaluated text results and their methods (master table plus techniques)

### Takeaway
On the DAIC-WOZ dev set, headline text macro-F1 values of 0.80-0.96 mostly depend on interviewer (Ellie) prompts and on a 35-session dev set. Under honest protocols the numbers are much lower. Participant-only models score about 0.59-0.72 macro-F1 on the official test set, and E-DAIC with subject-disjoint LOSO gives text-embedding AUROC 0.647 against 0.825 for a zero-shot LLM PHQ-8 scorer (and 0.768 for the hybrid). Our text AUC of ~0.74 under speaker-disjoint nested CV is therefore competitive. The biggest honest lever in the literature is LLM PHQ-8 item scoring used as a feature.

### Cited Findings
- **The dev-to-test collapse is the main honesty issue.** Watawana et al. report that P-GCN falls from 0.85 macro-F1 on DAIC-WOZ dev to 0.59 on the official test set. I-GCN falls from 0.88 to 0.62, and P-Longformer from 0.71 to 0.68. On E-DAIC test, the participant models score 0.40-0.54. — [Watawana et al. 2026 (LREC), code](https://github.com/idiap/bias_in_daic-woz/tree/main/LREC_2026)
- **The best honest E-DAIC anchor:** under LOSO on all 275 E-DAIC subjects, sentence-embedding text (mpnet + e5-large per turn, plus turn-structure features) reaches only AUROC 0.647 and mF1 0.621. A zero-shot gpt-oss-120b prompt that estimates the 8 PHQ items reaches AUROC 0.825, AP 0.666 and mF1 0.686. Combined (T+L), mF1 is 0.723 and AUROC 0.768. The authors call 0.723 the highest reported result under this protocol. — [Ishikawa & Duke 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.23977)
- The same audit found that cross-validation and official-test rankings of 96 E-DAIC configurations agree only moderately. The best CV configuration ranked 20th on the official test, top-3 overlap was zero, and the official-test winner was rank 1 in only 32.3% of subject bootstraps. — [Ishikawa & Duke 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.23977)
- The most comparable pipeline to ours is Gomez-Zaragoza et al. (Interspeech 2025), using the same j-hartmann emotion-english-roberta-large. Turn-level embeddings go into a GRU over context windows with speaker-independent 5-fold CV, with participant-level majority voting. Text F1-avg is 0.680 on E-DAIC (ASR), 0.595 on DAIC-WOZ and 0.699 on DAIC+. Emotion-fine-tuned RoBERTa beat plain RoBERTa, and fusion with HuBERT-er reached 0.840 on E-DAIC. — [Gomez-Zaragoza et al. 2025](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2025/gomezzaragoza25_interspeech.pdf)
- **Turn-level / item-level modeling:** Milintsevich et al.'s hierarchical model predicts the 8 PHQ items separately and sums them. It gives total MAE 3.78 against 5.03 for direct total-score regression on the DAIC-WOZ test, with binary macro-F1 73.9. — [Milintsevich et al. 2023](https://braininformatics.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40708-023-00185-9); search summary of [table 2](https://braininformatics.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40708-023-00185-9/tables/2)
- QuestMF (per-question models with an imbalanced ordinal loss) improves text-only CCC on E-DAIC from 0.591 (total-score model) to 0.615. — Mandal et al., "Enhancing Depression Detection via Question-wise Modality Fusion" (UKP Lab; local PDF, URL not recorded)
- Alhanai et al. (2018) show that sequence modeling of responses beats context-free text on DAIC-WOZ (F1 0.67 vs 0.59). Question-weighted text was worse (F1 0.44). — [Alhanai et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2018-2522)
- **LLM-extracted interpretable features plus linear regression** (Llama-3.1-70B scoring 23 questions) gave MAE 2.91 on the DAIC-WOZ held-out set and 2.86 on an external E-DAIC set (n=86). This is a rare external-validation result. — [Lee, Han, Woo 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12885269/)
- **Data augmentation:** PsyGAT trains with about 992 LLM-synthesized DAIC-WOZ sessions and about 996 E-DAIC sessions. It reports E-DAIC test mF1 71.37 (65.91 participant-only), on the official split, not CV. — [PsyGAT 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24126). SEGA uses LLM principle-guided augmentation and reports beating GPT-3.5/GPT-4 baselines. — [Chen et al. NAACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.452). Kang et al. 2024 claim LLM-synthetic synopses balance severity and improve prediction, with no numbers on the abstract page. — [arXiv 2411.17672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17672)
- **LLM summarization as preprocessing:** the BERT summarization approach reports F1 0.81 on validation but 0.67 on test. — [arXiv 2409.08483](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.08483)
- **Chain-of-thought / few-shot:** on DAIC-WOZ test, the expected gains from few-shot and CoT did not appear. Gemini-2.0-Flash went from mF1 0.84 zero-shot to 0.75 one-shot and 0.74 CoT; Mistral-7B from 0.78 to 0.60 two-shot and 0.70 CoT. CoT did give the lowest PHQ MAE (2.89, Llama-3.1-8B). — [Merzougui et al. 2025](https://maurelf.users.greyc.fr/docs/conferences/AIME_2025.pdf). Dep-LLM (theme-wise CoT plus entropy-based confidence) claims gains over zero-shot for nearly all of 21 LLMs. — [Lyu et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10796)

### Inferences
- Ranked by honest evidence, the text techniques with the largest gains are: (1) LLM PHQ-8 item scoring used as a feature or probability. It gave about +0.18 AUROC over sentence embeddings (0.825 vs 0.647) under E-DAIC LOSO, and it is the only technique validated under a protocol like ours. (2) Item-level (multi-target) prediction instead of a single total, which cut MAE from 5.03 to 3.78. (3) Turn-level sequence/context modeling instead of whole-document pooling: F1 0.67 vs 0.59 (Alhanai), and Gomez-Zaragoza's turn-window GRU at about 0.68 on E-DAIC. Few-shot, CoT, summarization and synthetic augmentation show no consistent honest gain. This matches our own Phase 8/8b null results for augmentation and few-shot.
- Our text AUC (~0.74, speaker-disjoint nested CV, Whisper-tiny) is above the 0.647 AUROC of the strict-LOSO sentence-embedding baseline on E-DAIC, but below the 0.825 of the zero-shot LLM item scorer. The most promising next text experiment is a locally run LLM producing PHQ-8 item scores as features fused with the ridge.
- Gains of a few F1 points on the 35-session dev set or 47/56-session test sets are within noise, given the bootstrap instability documented by Ishikawa & Duke.

### Gaps
- Original papers for rows 1-2, 4-10, 12 and 14 were not checked. Their numbers come from Burdisso 2024 / Alhanai 2018 / PsyGAT tables.
- I did not retrieve exact numbers for Dep-LLM, Kang 2024, or SEGA's own tables (only abstract pages).
- QuestMF's venue/year and URL were not recorded.
- I found no study that runs a text-only model and an LLM PHQ scorer side by side under CV on DAIC-WOZ (only E-DAIC LOSO in Ishikawa & Duke).

## Q2. How well do LLMs (zero-shot / fine-tuned) do on DAIC-WOZ, and which open models can run locally on a Mac CPU?

### Takeaway
Zero-shot LLMs given the whole transcript (with Ellie's turns) reach mF1 about 0.62-0.84 on the DAIC-WOZ test and 0.57-0.66 on E-DAIC. Fine-tuning (PEFT or a frozen backbone with a head) did not beat zero-shot in the one direct comparison. Open 7-8B models (Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B) score within a few points of frontier models and fit on a Mac in quantized form. 1B-4B models fail.

### Cited Findings
- On the DAIC-WOZ test (47), zero-shot binary mF1 was 0.84 for Gemini-2.0-Flash, 0.78 for Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, 0.69 for Llama-3.1-8B, 0.64 for Gemini-1.5-Pro and 0.62 for DeepSeek-R1-Distill-8B. The prior SOTA reference was Agarwal et al. at 0.81. — [Merzougui et al. 2025 (AIME)](https://maurelf.users.greyc.fr/docs/conferences/AIME_2025.pdf)
- In that study, in-context learning beat fine-tuning overall. The best fine-tuned model was frozen Mistral-7B with a shallow head (mF1 0.71). PEFT Mistral reached 0.64, Llama-3.2-1B 0.41 and ModernBERT 0.34. Model size within the ICL setting did not improve performance (Mistral-7B and Llama-8B were about as good as Gemini-1.5-Pro). The authors note that Llama-3.2-1B and the 7-8B models are compact enough for edge devices. — [Merzougui et al. 2025](https://maurelf.users.greyc.fr/docs/conferences/AIME_2025.pdf)
- Per-item PHQ-8 F1 from LLMs is weak (about 0.15-0.60 per item). — [Merzougui 2025, Table 4](https://maurelf.users.greyc.fr/docs/conferences/AIME_2025.pdf). Teferra et al. found GPT-4o best overall (binary accuracy 75.9%, F1 0.74, MCC 0.25 on 100 sessions). Llama-3-8B was best on anhedonia (Likert accuracy 52%, F1 0.50) and on the "failure" item (MCC 0.49). — [Teferra et al. 2025](https://journals.plos.org/digitalhealth/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pdig.0000943)
- Under E-DAIC LOSO, zero-shot gpt-oss-120b (deterministic, PHQ-8 item JSON prompt, head-tail truncation to 12k tokens, ASR full-dialogue transcripts) gave AUROC 0.825. Its fold-tuned threshold was very stable (about 0.15). — [Ishikawa & Duke 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.23977)
- LLM baselines (zero-shot, with patient text and therapist questions) scored on DAIC dev / E-DAIC test mF1: GPT-5 76.31 / 66.31; Qwen2.5-Omni-7B 74.32 / 59.71; Kimi-K2 72.63 / 58.90; DeepSeek-v3.2 69.67 / 58.90; Qwen3-Omni-30B 69.54 / 56.75; Gemma3-4B 26.67 / 22.91 (degenerate). — [PsyGAT 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24126)
- Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct, used zero-shot as a feature extractor (23 scores 0-10 into linear regression), gave PHQ-8 MAE 2.91 and 2.86 externally. — [Lee et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12885269/)
- Contamination caveat: the audit authors note they cannot rule out DAIC transcripts in LLM pretraining corpora, though they argue it is less plausible because the corpora are access-controlled. — [Ishikawa & Duke 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.23977)

### Inferences
- On a Mac CPU, quantized (4-bit GGUF via llama.cpp/Ollama) Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct should be practical for a one-off scoring pass over 272 transcripts. They need roughly 5 GB of RAM, and DAIC transcripts are a few thousand tokens. Prompt processing on CPU is slow (likely minutes per transcript), so expect hours, not days. This estimate is mine, not from a source. gpt-oss-120b and Llama-70B are impractical on CPU. Avoid models of 4B or smaller: Gemma3-4B and Llama-3.2-1B collapsed.
- Use greedy/deterministic decoding and a PHQ-8 item JSON prompt (the Ishikawa prompt is reproduced verbatim in the paper). Feed the LLM score into the existing ridge/fusion as an extra feature inside the CV folds. Choose the threshold within each fold.
- Feeding the full dialogue including Ellie (as all these LLM studies do) risks the interviewer shortcut in Q4. A participant-only ablation is needed for honesty.

### Gaps
- No source reported CPU runtimes for these models on DAIC transcripts.
- I found no LLM study that reports AUC under speaker-disjoint CV on DAIC-WOZ itself, and no participant-only versus full-dialogue LLM ablation.
- Ohse et al. (Interspeech 2024, zero-shot LLM) could not be located.

## Q3. How much do results depend on manual vs ASR transcripts?

### Takeaway
There is little direct, quantified evidence. Available results suggest that modern ASR (WER about 14-15%) costs little for document-level text classification. E-DAIC's provided ASR transcripts are poor enough that several groups re-transcribe with Whisper. Whisper-tiny's higher WER is an untested risk for our pipeline.

### Cited Findings
- On DAIC-WOZ, automatic transcriptions caused only a slight performance drop relative to manual transcriptions (Lopez-Otero et al., Interspeech 2017; exact numbers not in the abstract). — [ISCA archive](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2017/lopezotero17b_interspeech.html)
- E-DAIC's provided transcripts have substantial errors. Lee et al. re-transcribed with Whisper large-v3 plus manual correction. — [Lee et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12885269/). Sadeghi et al. (BHI 2023) report that the Whisper API gives considerably higher-quality transcripts than E-DAIC's own. — [BHI 2023](https://bhiconference.github.io/BHI2023/2023/pdfs/1570918113.pdf)
- Watawana et al.'s ASR pipeline gives a 15.3% WER on the E-DAIC participant side, measured against the provided transcripts. — [Watawana et al. 2026](https://github.com/idiap/bias_in_daic-woz/tree/main/LREC_2026). A search summary of a 2025 Interspeech DAIC-WOZ study reports Whisper-large-v2 at 14.5% WER. Maji et al. (Interspeech 2025) argue that ASR transcripts of moderate quality are still useful for depression detection. — [Maji et al. 2025](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2025/maji25_interspeech.pdf)
- Indirect comparison: the same RoBERTa-large-er pipeline scored text F1 0.680 on E-DAIC (Google ASR) and 0.595 on DAIC-WOZ (manual). Datasets differ, so this does not show an ASR penalty. — [Gomez-Zaragoza et al. 2025](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2025/gomezzaragoza25_interspeech.pdf)
- Outside DAIC: models trained on manual transcripts degrade when tested on ASR (WER about 20%), and training on ASR partly offsets this. — [arXiv 1906.02239](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.02239). For psychiatric interviews, the best ASR had median WER 8.9% against 7.6% for human transcription. — [JMIR Mental Health 2023](https://mental.jmir.org/2023/1/e48517)

### Inferences
- Because we train and test on the same ASR (Whisper-tiny), the train/test mismatch penalty does not apply. A cheap check is to re-transcribe with Whisper-small/medium/large-v3 and see whether text AUC moves. Better ASR mainly helps an LLM PHQ scorer, which depends on exact content such as "diagnosed with PTSD".
- Whisper (ASR) produces a full dialogue including Ellie's turns. E-DAIC's official text contains only the participant side, so our ASR transcripts may carry interviewer content unless it is filtered. This matters for Q4.

## Q4. Which question-level features matter most?

### Takeaway
The most predictive interview regions are the late questions about past mental-health history ("have you been diagnosed with depression/PTSD", "have you been in therapy", follow-ups). Much of that signal comes from Ellie's script and follow-up branching rather than participant language, so it can be a shortcut. Among symptoms, depressed mood, negative sentiment and sleep are the strongest LLM-scored features.

### Cited Findings
- In DAIC-WOZ, most depressed-group keywords cluster in a late segment where Ellie asks about past mental-health experiences. An affirmative answer to "Have you been diagnosed with depression?" triggers extra follow-up prompts. Fine-tuning E-longBERT on only the first half of the interview dropped F1 to 0.60, while the second half alone matched the full interview (0.84). Interviewer-prompt models therefore exploit "discriminative shortcuts". — [Burdisso et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.14463)
- The interviewer bias holds across datasets and architectures: interviewer-only models reach high scores without participant language (ANDROIDS I-Longformer 0.98 mF1, 19 points over participant-only). Participant-only models spread evidence across the whole interview. — [Watawana et al. 2026](https://github.com/idiap/bias_in_daic-woz/tree/main/LREC_2026)
- For LLM features (Llama-3.1-70B), the strongest correlates of PHQ-8 were depressed mood (r≈0.89), negative sentiment (0.83), sleep disturbance (0.82), positive sentiment (-0.82) and the "mental filter" cognitive distortion (0.82). — [Lee et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12885269/)
- Per-item estimation is hardest for sleep (highest item MAE, 0.75) and easiest for anhedonia (0.47) on the DAIC-WOZ dev set. Somatic items tend to be underestimated and psychomotor change overestimated. — [EmoTrack 2026 (search summary)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.22286). LLMs do best on emotional and cognitive items. — [Teferra et al. 2025](https://journals.plos.org/digitalhealth/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pdig.0000943)
- Topic sensitivity: E-DAIC text-model scores rise sharply on symptom-dense interview slices, while audio scores stay nearly flat. The text-minus-audio gap was positive across all 5 seeds. — [Ishikawa & Duke 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.23977)
- Gong & Poellabauer's topic-modeling approach (question-topic-specific features) reached F1 0.70 on the DAIC-WOZ dev set (ensemble). — [via Alhanai 2018 table](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2018-2522)

### Inferences
- For our pipeline, mean-pooling the whole ASR transcript likely includes Ellie's follow-up prompts, which leak diagnosis-branching information. Ablate interviewer turns (participant-only pooling) to quantify how much of AUC 0.74 is shortcut. Then try topic-specific pooled embeddings (mental-health history, sleep, mood/feelings, therapy) as separate feature blocks under the same nested CV.
- Answers to the "diagnosed with depression/PTSD" and "therapy" questions are partly a legitimate clinical signal, since the participant discloses history. Report results with and without them.

### Gaps
- I found no published ranking of individual DAIC-WOZ question topics by honest (CV, participant-only) predictive value. Evidence is keyword heatmaps (Burdisso/Watawana) and LLM feature correlations (Lee).
